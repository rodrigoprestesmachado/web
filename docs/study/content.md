## Do not try to study everything at once
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:100px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* Organize your study by distributing the topics throughout the weeks in a
balanced way. A practical tip is to track your progress in the subjects through
weekly exercises, allowing for continuous and consistent learning.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->


<!-- .slide: data-background="#D6F3FF" data-transition="convex" -->
## Questions for reflection
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:100px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* How can you structure a study plan to overcome the challenges of the subject?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* How can you monitor your progress week by week? What are the signs that you may need help?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* What lessons learned this week can be adapted and applied in the upcoming weeks?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

## Study efficiently
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:80px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* Structure your study sessions by alternating periods of intense focus with
breaks. During focus periods, it is essential to avoid distractions such as
social media, replying to messages, or even listening to music, save these
activities for the breaks.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* An effective technique is the Pomodoro method: study for 25 minutes intensely,
followed by a 5-minute break. If 25 minutes feels too challenging, start with 15
minutes of focus and 5 minutes of rest.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Whenever possible, switch up the study topics during these focus periods. For
example, if you have just reviewed Java collections, in the next focus block,
try switching to Java exceptions. This variation helps keep your brain active
and reinforces learning.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->


<!-- .slide: data-background="#DDFDAF" data-transition="convex" -->
## Questions for reflection
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:80px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* Are you managing your focus and rest periods effectively?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* What strategies can help you minimize distractions during focus time?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Is there a sequence or pattern that works best for you, such as studying a
concept in one focus block and practicing it in another?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

## Use AI smartly
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:80px; font-size: 38px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* Instead of directly asking AI how to solve a code or exercise, try solving the
problem on your own first. Afterward, you can ask the AI to evaluate your
solution, correct any errors, and clarify points of doubt. This way, you better
consolidate the learning and avoid letting the AI think for you.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->


<!-- .slide: data-background="#FEFDE5" data-transition="convex" -->
## Questions for reflection
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:80px; font-size: 38px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* How can you use AI as a support tool rather than a substitute for developing
your own thinking?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* How can you break a problem into smaller subproblems? Can you identify parts
that you already know how to solve?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Which part of the problem is most challenging? How can you tackle it in
isolation?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Can AI correct or improve the initial solution you created?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

## Monitor your feelings
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:80px; font-size: 38px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* When we solve small problems, it makes us feel confident and motivated to
continue.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* However, when we study, it is normal to encounter difficulties, which can
generate negative feelings such as frustration or lack of motivation.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Therefore, constantly monitor the negative feelings that are being generated during the study process.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Always ask for help if you notice that the difficulties in the subject are generating negative feelings to the point that you cannot deal with them alone.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->


<!-- .slide: data-background="#FEECE5" data-transition="convex" -->
## Questions for reflection
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:80px; font-size: 38px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* How often do you take a step back to reflect on your emotions during study
sessions? What can you do to regularly monitor and adjust your emotional state
while learning?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* What are some positive actions you can take when you feel stuck or frustrated
during your studies? Could a short break, a change of perspective, or talking to
someone make a difference?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Can you recall a time when asking for help made a big difference in how you
felt about a difficult subject? How did getting support affect your feelings of
frustration or motivation?
