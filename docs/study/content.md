## Do not try to study everything at once
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:30px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

The weekly study plan.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:30px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* ⚠️ Studying everything at once **before a test** can be overwhelming and hinder
your ability to explore each topic with the needed depth and focus.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:50px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* ✏️ Organize your studies by distributing topics evenly throughout the weeks.
Create a balanced **study plan** that includes dedicated weekly study sessions.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:30px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

    * 💡 Track your progress in each subject through weekly exercises to
promote **continuous** and consistent learning.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:50px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

    * 🤖 You can use Artificial Intelligence (AI) to create a personalized study
plan that suits your needs. Just provide your courses, topics of study, the days
and hours you are available, and your preferred break times. AI can then
generate a customized study schedule for you. Give it a try!
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:50px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

Consistently track your progress.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:30px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* ⚠️ Creating a study plan is a good starting point. Still, it is essential to
monitor and evaluate your progress every week.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:50px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Examples of questions to ask yourself about a homework, can be:
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:50px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

| **Aspect**    | **Questions** (Gurung, Dunlosky, 2023)                       |
|---------------|--------------------------------------------------------------|
| **Plan**      | What is the instructor’s goal in having me do this task?
| **Monitor**   | What strategies am I using that are working well or not working well to help me learn?
| **Evaluating**| To what extent did I successfully accomplish the goals of the task?

  * I generally return phone calls/messages promptly.

## Study efficiently
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:80px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* Structure your study sessions by alternating periods of **intense focus** with **breaks**.
During focus periods, it is essential to avoid distractions such as social media,
replying to messages, or even listening to music; save these activities for the
breaks.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* An effective technique is the **Pomodoro method**: focus intensely for 25
minutes, then take a 5-minute break. If 25 minutes feels too challenging, begin
with 15 minutes of focus followed by 5 minutes of rest.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Whenever possible, change the study topics during your focus periods. For
example, if you finished reviewing Object Orientation, try switching to Java
Control of Errors for the next focus block. This variation engages your brain,
facilitates problem recognition, and reinforces learning.
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:75px; font-size: 25px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->


<!-- .slide: data-background="#DDFDAF" data-transition="convex" -->
## Questions for reflection
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:80px; font-size: 35px; color:black; font-family: Comic Sans MS;" -->

* Are you managing your focus and rest periods effectively?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:60px; font-size: 30px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* What strategies can help you minimize distractions during focus time?
<!-- .element: style="margin-bottom:60px; font-size: 30px; color:black; font-family:Optima;" -->

* Is there a particular sequence or pattern that works best for you? For
example, can we study one concept in a focused block and then practice it in
another?
